# Writing style rules for the final report

Working list. The goal is prose that reads like a person wrote it: plain, occasionally uneven,
committed to its claims. Not a keyword blacklist to apply mechanically, because scrubbing banned
words while keeping the underlying rhythm just produces a different kind of artificial text.

**The principle:** no single feature gives writing away. Density and clustering do. A paper with
four em dashes reads normally; the same paper with sixty does not.

---

## 1. Punctuation and mechanics

| # | Rule | Draft-1 count | Target |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1.1 | **Em dashes (`---`).** The loudest single tell. Replace with a comma, a full stop, parentheses, or restructure the sentence. | 62 | ≤ 6 |
| 1.2 | **Semicolons.** Most should be full stops. Two short sentences beat one balanced compound. | 29 | ≤ 10 |
| 1.3 | **Italics (`\emph{}`).** Reserve for technical terms at first use and for variable names. Never for rhetorical stress. | 64 | ≤ 20 |
| 1.4 | No ellipses, no arrows (`→`) inside prose sentences, no exclamation marks. |  |  |
| 1.5 | Parenthetical asides are fine and under-used. They are a normal human alternative to the dash. |  |  |

## 2. Sentence-level habits to avoid

- **2.1 Negative parallelism.** "Not X, but Y." "It is not X; it is Y." "The decisive factor was
  not architecture but representation." State the positive claim directly and move on.
- **2.2 Tricolons.** Three parallel items, endlessly. Vary the count: two items, or four, or a
  plain sentence. Watch especially the opening of sections.
- **2.3 Appositive stacking.** "The input, two distance matrices, both normalised, is..." Break it
  into separate sentences.
- **2.4 Closing aphorisms.** The punchy one-line summary that ends a paragraph. Cut most of them.
  Paragraphs are allowed to just stop.
- **2.5 Repeated connectives.** "rather than" appeared 12 times in draft 1. Vary or drop.
- **2.6 Hedging stacks.** "may", "could", "arguably", "somewhat", "generally", "tends to". State
  the finding and its actual uncertainty, then stop hedging.
- **2.7 Intensifier tics.** "precisely", "exactly", "genuinely", "deliberately", "crucially",
  "notably", "importantly", "critically". Usually deletable with no loss.
- **2.8 Over-signposting.** "As we will see", "As noted above", "In what follows". A reader can
  find Section 6 without being told twice.

## 3. Vocabulary to avoid

delve, leverage (as a verb), showcase, underscore, pivotal, seamless, testament, landscape, realm,
tapestry, nuanced, holistic, meticulous, intricate, garner, foster, myriad, plethora, harness,
unlock, elevate, transformative, game-changing, cutting-edge, boasts, navigate (figuratively),
embark, illuminate, shed light on, at its core, in essence, in today's world, it is worth noting,
it is important to note, that said, moreover, furthermore, additionally, ultimately, in short.

**Allowed with care:** "robust" and "state of the art" have real technical meanings here. Use them
only in those senses.

## 4. Structure

- **4.1 Bolded paragraph lead-ins.** A legitimate convention, but wall-to-wall use is a tell.
  Keep them where they carry real structure (the D1–D5 findings, the any-(N,M) mechanism); drop
  them elsewhere.
- **4.2 Paragraph length.** Vary it on purpose. Four paragraphs of near-identical length in a row
  reads machine-made. Some should be two sentences.
- **4.3 Section endings.** Not every section needs a summarising final sentence.
- **4.4 Lists.** Prose is the default in a paper. Keep lists for the contributions, the research
  questions, and genuinely enumerable things.
- **4.5 Sentence length.** Mix short and long. Uniform medium-length sentences are the giveaway
  most people can feel but not name.

## 5. Voice

- **5.1** Commit to claims. Academic prose is allowed to be flat and even slightly dry.
- **5.2** First person plural ("we trained", "we expect") is standard and natural here. Use it.
- **5.3** Do not explain the obvious. Trust the reader.
- **5.4** Prefer concrete numbers to qualitative flourish. "1.048 versus 1.052" beats "markedly
  better".
- **5.5** Slight unevenness is a feature. A sentence that is a little long, or a transition that
  is a little abrupt, reads more human than uniform polish.
